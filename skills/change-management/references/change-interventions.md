# Select change interventions

## Organize a transformation

For a broad program, connect the case for change, credible sponsorship, the proposed future way of working, practical enablement, evidence from early use, and sustained operating ownership. This work can overlap and repeat. Choose phases and timing based on dependencies and capacity; do not force a standard five-stage calendar.

A sponsor needs explicit decisions to own and time to resolve barriers. A guiding group should include operating knowledge and affected voices, not only rank. Test an early win against baseline and avoid attributing all improvement to the program if other factors changed.

## Match a response to the signal

| Observed signal | Investigate | Possible intervention |
|---|---|---|
| People cannot explain the purpose | Conflicting messages, relevance to their work, language or access barriers | Clarify the specific change and consequence; equip managers and test comprehension. |
| People understand but do not support it | Incentives, workload, trust, legitimate job or service concerns | Address the underlying cost or trade-off, change the design if warranted, and communicate what is still undecided. |
| Trained users still cannot complete tasks | Access, usability, missing features, task complexity, opportunity to practice | Fix the workflow or tool, provide practice and task-level support, observe completion quality. |
| Old processes persist | Missing functionality, audit requirements, transition safety, informal incentives | Compare the old and new paths; retire duplication only after the new path meets the need. |
| Early use fades | Manager behavior, workload, reliability, reinforcement, usefulness | Integrate the behavior into the operating routine and correct defects; do not assume reminders alone will work. |

Do not label a person resistant from one missed meeting or infer motivation from low usage. Quote or summarize observed evidence accurately and keep sensitive stakeholder analysis within the engagement's sharing rules.

## Communications

Design for each audience's practical questions: what changes, why, what is decided, what remains open, what it means for their work, when it happens, and where they can get help or influence the design. Use accessible language and channels the audience can actually use.

For AI and automation, state employment and accountability implications honestly. Do not promise augmentation rather than replacement unless that is the confirmed plan. Explain what people must review, when to override the tool, and how to report failures where those affect the workflow. Obtain actual authorization for communications and commitments rather than treating a draft plan as permission to send.

## Champions and enablement

Choose credible peers with relevant work knowledge, protected time, training, and a route to resolve problems. A champion network without capacity should not be presented as a staffed delivery mechanism.

Train against tasks and role-specific proficiency, with practice, feedback, job aids, and support at the point of use. Adapt delivery for access, language, schedule, and disability needs. Measure task success and errors after training; completion is evidence of attendance, not proficiency.

## Reinforcement

Assign operating ownership, support, metrics, and policy changes before the project team departs. Tie reinforcement to the desired behavior without creating incentives to hide failures or inflate adoption. Define the trigger for improving the solution, increasing support, slowing rollout, or changing approach. Report sustained performance and business effect separately.
